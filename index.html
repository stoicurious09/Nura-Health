Why this exists

On 3 March 2026, Neelam had a severe heart attack. She survived — four stents, an angiography that stressed her kidneys, a body that needed a cardiologist, a nephrologist, a hepatologist, and a family with no medical training to hold the whole picture together. Her son had a laptop and no idea what a rising creatinine meant.

That gap — between what a specialist knows about one organ and what a family needs to know about a whole person — is where NURA lives. Not a chatbot. Not a records drawer. A curated, cited, evidence-graded intelligence layer that explains what's happening, tracks what's trending, and prepares a family for the next conversation with the person who can actually help: the doctor.

— for Neelam, and every family sitting in a hospital corridor trying to understand.
