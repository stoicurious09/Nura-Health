NURA · Execution Playbook · August 2026

From one family's binder of reports to a product — in phases, on a critical path, under ₹5 lakh.

Offering, feature roadmap, pricing, budget, and the week-by-week plan. Companion document: NURA Strategy Report (market evidence behind every choice here).

1 · The fundamental offering

The hook — one sentence a worried son or daughter instantly understands:

"Send us your parent's medical reports on WhatsApp. Within a day, you'll understand what's going on in their body — and walk into the next doctor's visit prepared."

Free taste: a plain-language Health Picture from their reports. The paid product: NURA keeps watching — trends across every organ system, medication schedules, a prep sheet before every consultation, and quiet alerts when something drifts. Every artifact ends with "questions to ask your doctor" — NURA prepares families, it never replaces doctors.

Why this hook: it demands zero installs (WhatsApp), zero behaviour change, monetises the exact moment of highest motivation (a health crisis), and every report uploaded deepens the proprietary family-data moat. It is the productised version of what your family needed on the day Neelam came home.

2 · Feature architecture

Everything maps to one engine — ingest family health data → structure it → make it intelligible → make it anticipatory. The intelligible/anticipatory half is not a chatbot: it is the Biomarker Evidence Compendium — curated, multi-source-corroborated, citation-carrying clinical evidence — compiled into deterministic rules (thresholds, trends, cadences), with the AI model doing only extraction and plain-language translation. Use cases (post-op, newborn, preventive, dialysis) are evidence packs added to that engine in order of urgency-to-pay.

Concierge validation — "NURA Care," delivered by hand

15–20 families, WhatsApp + Claude-assisted workflows + a doctor advisor reviewing every output. You are the product; the software comes later. Every claim in the Strategy Report's validation gauntlet (A1–A5) gets its answer here.

Native apps, use-case packs (newborn, preventive, dialysis), doctor share links, overseas push. By now the pitch to investors is: validated retention, mishaps-averted stories, and a proprietary family-data asset.

5 · The input ladder — from monthly reports to daily life

The engagement physics NURA must respect: lab reports arrive monthly at best — a labs-only product gets opened monthly and quietly churns. Recovery, though, happens daily: medicines, walks, meals, sleep, cigarettes not smoked. Daily inputs are simultaneously the retention loop (the reason to open NURA every day), the correlation dataset (lifestyle in, biomarker trends out — the deep intelligence), and the evidence base's biggest levers (quitting smoking cuts post-MI mortality ~46%; every +1,000 daily steps ~15% — see Compendium §8b). The data strategy climbs a ladder, one rung per phase, never asking for more than the family's habit can carry:

Two disciplines keep this honest: self-reported data is noisy, so the engine weights device-captured and lab-verified data above taps; and the check-in burden is a product surface — every added question must buy its keep in retention or correlation value, or it gets cut. 9 · Unit economics sketch (to validate, not to worship)

  • CAC: target near-zero in Year 1 — cardiac support groups, discharge-desk word of mouth, doctor share links, founder story. The payment test tells you if a family referred by another family converts; that referral loop is the business model.
  • ARPU: blended ₹2,500/yr assumption (mix of ₹2,499 Family, ₹1,499/mo Care, and $59 NRI plans). 500 paying families ≈ ₹12.5L ARR — sustainability for a solo founder; 3,000 ≈ ₹75L ARR — a fundable seed story.
  • Retention is the truth-teller: post-op urgency fades by month 6–9. The product must convert crisis users into ambient family-vault users (new members added, preventive packs) — measure month-6 retention above all else.
