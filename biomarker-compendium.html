NURA · Biomarker Evidence Compendium · v1 · August 2026

What the numbers mean — and what the evidence says they mean for a life.

The knowledge core of NURA's intelligence engine: peer-reviewed, guideline-grade correlations between test markers and risk, recovery, longevity, and fatality — written so a layperson understands and a junior doctor still finds it rigorous. v1 goes deep on the post-op cardiac journey (Neelam's panel); every other system carries a skeleton to be filled. This document doubles as the NotebookLM source pack core.

How to read this — and what NURA is allowed to do with it. Every claim carries an evidence grade: A clinical guideline or meta-analysis of trials · B large cohort/consortium evidence · C emerging or single-study evidence. "Red flags" are conversation triggers, not diagnoses. NURA is an information and organization tool: it explains, tracks trends, and prepares questions — it never diagnoses, prescribes, or advises changing treatment. Every insight ships with "discuss with your doctor."

The first principle of the engine: a trend beats a value. Nearly every marker below predicts outcomes more powerfully as a direction and rate of change than as a single reading — chronic-phase NT-proBNP beats admission values, HbA1c swings carry risk beyond level, a 25% creatinine rise means more than any one number. Most of medicine's data is read one report at a time; NURA's entire reason to exist is reading the line, not the dot.

1 · Cardiac stress & injury the heart's own signals

NT-proBNP / BNP

the heart's "strain gauge" — a hormone released when heart walls are stretched
RangesRoughly <125 pg/mL (NT-proBNP, under 75y) is reassuring; post-MI values are read against trend, not one cut-off — prognostic cut-offs vary with age, BMI and kidney function.
EvidenceA Independently predicts death after heart attack — HR ≈ 1.8 per standard-deviation rise; in the PARADISE-MI trial it predicted mortality about as well as full clinical risk scores (GRACE) by itself. B Values measured in the stable phase weeks after the event predict long-term outcomes better than values from the acute admission — the trend as things settle is the signal.
Red flagsA rising level across serial tests after discharge; a level that fails to fall as recovery proceeds. Either is a "call the cardiologist" trend.
What helpsMedication adherence (the post-MI regimen exists to unload the heart), fluid/salt discipline, cardiac rehab. Discuss any rise with the treating cardiologist.
CadenceTypically checked at follow-ups and whenever breathlessness/swelling appears; serial values are the point.
SourcesPARADISE-MI, Circ HF · AMI cohort, PMC · Serial NT-proBNP post-MI

High-sensitivity troponin

the heart-muscle injury marker — released when cardiac cells are damaged
RangesAssay-specific (99th-percentile cut-off). After a heart attack it is expected to be elevated and then fall over days–weeks.
EvidenceA The defining marker of myocardial injury in universal MI definitions; persistent or re-rising elevation after the expected decline signals new injury or ongoing strain and predicts worse outcomes.
Red flagsA new rise after the post-event decline, especially with chest symptoms — emergency-track, not "wait for the next appointment."
CadenceNot routinely tracked at home; appears in reports during events and admissions — NURA's job is to place each value on the injury/recovery timeline so families understand it.

2 · Lipids & residual risk what rebuilds the blockage

LDL cholesterol ("bad cholesterol")

the particle count that drives artery-wall plaque — the #1 modifiable number after a stent
TargetsAfter a heart attack/stent (very-high-risk): <55 mg/dL and ≥50% reduction from baseline; ≤40 mg/dL if a second event occurs within 2 years (ESC/EAS; 2025 focused update reaffirms). General-population "normal" printed on lab reports is not the post-stent target — this single confusion causes silent undertreatment.
EvidenceA Guideline-anchored, backed by trial meta-analysis: every ~39 mg/dL LDL reduction cuts major vascular events ~22%. Secondary-prevention populations routinely miss targets — therapeutic inertia is documented across registries, which is exactly the gap a family dashboard closes by asking "are we at target?" at every review.
Red flagsLDL above 55 mg/dL on any post-stent report; a drift upward across two reports (adherence, dose, or diet has slipped).
What helpsStatin adherence (the single highest-leverage habit post-stent), diet pattern, and — via the doctor — dose escalation, ezetimibe, or newer agents (bempedoic acid, PCSK9i) if targets aren't met.
Cadence4–12 weeks after any therapy change, then every 3–12 months once stable.
Sources2025 ESC/EAS focused update · 2026 ACC/AHA dyslipidemia guideline · Therapeutic inertia, ITACARE-P

ApoB & Lp(a)

the deeper particle-level truth behind cholesterol numbers
RangesApoB: secondary target once LDL is at goal (very-high-risk goal <65 mg/dL, ESC). Lp(a): largely genetic — measure once in a lifetime; >50 mg/dL (~125 nmol/L) marks added risk.
EvidenceA ApoB counts the actual number of artery-damaging particles and catches residual risk that a "normal" LDL can hide — especially with diabetes or high triglycerides. A 2025 ESC/EAS and 2026 ACC/AHA guidance both elevate Lp(a) to a once-in-life screening test.
Red flagsNever-measured Lp(a) in a cardiac family — a one-time ₹1–2K test that reclassifies the whole family's risk (it's heritable; siblings and children should know).
SourcesACC/AHA 2026 · Family Heart — 2025 guideline summary

hs-CRP

the inflammation smoulder-detector — how "angry" the arteries still are
Ranges<1 mg/L low risk · 1–3 average · ≥2 mg/L on statin therapy = "residual inflammatory risk" (measure when otherwise well — infection spikes it).
EvidenceA Landmark 2023 analysis of three trials (31,000+ statin-treated patients): inflammation (hs-CRP) predicted cardiovascular death and all-cause death more strongly than remaining cholesterol — top-quartile hs-CRP carried HR 2.68 for CV mortality. B ~38% of treated post-MI patients still carry hs-CRP ≥2 despite controlled LDL (MACE HR ≈ 1.8). This is a marker most Indian follow-up panels don't include — a family that asks for it is ahead of the system.
What helpsExercise/cardiac rehab, weight, sleep, glycemic control; treating dental/gut sources of inflammation; emerging anti-inflammatory therapies exist — a cardiologist conversation, flagged by NURA.
CadenceWith the lipid panel, 1–2×/year when stable.
SourcesRidker et al., Lancet 2023 · Eur J Prev Cardiol 2026 · Post-STEMI residual risk, Frontiers

3 · Kidney the organ that quietly pays for the heart's crisis

Creatinine → eGFR

the kidney's filtration score — how much cleaning capacity remains
RangeseGFR ≥90 normal · 60–89 mildly reduced · <60 for 3+ months = chronic kidney disease · <30 specialist territory. Always read alongside uACR (below) on the KDIGO risk grid.
EvidenceB CKD Prognosis Consortium (millions of participants): mortality risk vs eGFR 95 — HR 1.03 at 60, 1.38 at 45, 3.11 at 15. Kidney and heart risk compound multiplicatively with albuminuria. A Post-angiography: contrast dye can injure kidneys — a creatinine rise of ≥0.5 mg/dL or ≥25% within 48–72h of the procedure defines contrast-induced nephropathy (~7% incidence; in-hospital mortality 9.7% vs 0.5% without). This is precisely the multi-organ cascade Neelam's family lived.
Red flagsAny 25%+ creatinine rise — after contrast procedures, after new medicines (see potassium card), or during illness/dehydration. Three consecutive small rises = a trend conversation with the nephrologist.
What helpsHydration discipline, avoiding NSAID painkillers (a silent kidney threat common in Indian households), BP and sugar control, medication-dose review with declining eGFR.
Cadence48–72h after any contrast procedure; within 1 week of starting/up-titrating ACE/ARB; then every 3–6 months, tighter if declining.
SourcesCKD-PC meta-analysis, Lancet · KDIGO risk categories primer · Contrast nephropathy after PCI

Urine albumin (uACR)

protein leaking into urine — the earliest whisper of kidney and vessel damage
Ranges<30 mg/g normal · 30–300 moderately increased · >300 severely increased.
EvidenceB Risk rises log-linearly with no safe threshold: vs uACR 5, mortality HR 1.08 at 10, 1.38 at 30, 2.16 at 300 — and it multiplies with low eGFR. One of the cheapest, most under-ordered tests in Indian follow-up care.
Red flagsNever tested despite diabetes/hypertension/cardiac disease; any category jump between tests.
CadenceAt least yearly in cardiac/diabetic/hypertensive members; 6-monthly if elevated.
SourcesKidney International meta-analysis · KDIGO 2024 / KDOQI commentary

Potassium & sodium

the heart's electrical chemistry — kept in a narrow band by the very drugs that protect the heart
RangesK⁺ 3.5–5.0 mmol/L (heart-failure care aims for the middle); Na⁺ 135–145 mmol/L.
EvidenceA Mortality is U-shaped: both high potassium (a known effect of ACE-inhibitors/ARBs/spironolactone, worse with kidney impairment or diabetes) and low potassium (classically from diuretics; can trigger dangerous rhythms) carry excess mortality — JACC state-of-the-art review. The post-MI drug regimen itself is why this needs watching: protection and risk come in the same tablet.
Red flagsK⁺ outside 3.5–5.0, especially after a medication start/change or a bout of vomiting/diarrhoea/dehydration.
CadenceA Within 1 week of starting/up-titrating ACE/ARB/MRA · monthly for 3 months · then quarterly for the first year — a cadence most families never learn, and the clearest example of NURA turning guidelines into reminders.
SourcesJACC review — potassium in HF · Cleveland Clinic J Med — RAAS monitoring · Monitoring after RAAS initiation

4 · Liver the drug-processing plant under new load

ALT / AST

liver-cell leak markers — the liver's response to new medications and congestion
RangesLab-specific (ALT roughly <35–40 U/L). On statins, the action threshold is >3× the upper limit of normal — mild fluctuation is common and usually benign.
EvidenceA Statin practice: baseline ALT before starting, recheck 4–12 weeks after initiation or dose change, then annually; serious statin liver injury is rare. Post-cardiac patients juggle multiple hepatically-cleared drugs — the trend across the whole regimen is what matters, not one enzyme in isolation.
Red flags>3× upper limit, or steadily climbing values with fatigue/jaundice/dark urine — medication review with the doctor, not silent discontinuation (stopping a statin unsupervised carries its own cardiac risk).
SourcesLiverTox — atorvastatin · Statin liver monitoring practice

GGT & bilirubin

bile-duct stress and pigment clearance — and quiet whole-body risk dials
EvidenceB GGT predicts cardiovascular mortality in dose-response fashion even within the normal range — highest vs lowest levels HR 1.59; ~10% added risk per 10 U/L (527K-participant meta-analysis). Gallbladder issues (Neelam's case) show up here first. Bilirubin elevation with GGT points to bile flow; isolated mild bilirubin (Gilbert's) is common and benign.
Red flagsRising GGT + rising bilirubin together — bile-duct/gallbladder conversation; sudden jaundice is urgent.
SourcesGGT dose-response meta-analysis, PLOS One · GGT and all-cause mortality cohort

Albumin

the body's reserve-strength protein — nutrition, liver output, and inflammation in one number
Ranges3.5–5.0 g/dL; <3.5 is hypoalbuminemia.
EvidenceB In stable coronary disease, low albumin marked a 10.2% vs 0.5% all-cause mortality difference; in heart failure, 1-year survival 66% vs 83% (adjusted mortality HR ≈ 2.2). One of the nine PhenoAge biological-age inputs. In an elderly post-op parent, a falling albumin is often the earliest visible signature of declining reserve — eminently actionable through nutrition.
Red flags<3.5 g/dL, or a steady down-trend across reports — nutrition/protein-intake conversation with the doctor or dietitian.
SourcesAlbumin in stable CHD · Albumin in chronic HF

5 · Sugar the slow-burn accelerant

HbA1c & fasting glucose

the 3-month sugar average — and post-MI, a marker where "lower" is not always "better"
Ranges<5.7% normal · 5.7–6.4 prediabetes · ≥6.5 diabetes. Post-MI with diabetes, the mortality-optimal band is ~5.5–6.5%, individualized by the doctor.
EvidenceB U-shaped mortality after heart attack: vs 5.5–6.5%, adjusted odds 1.89 at <5.5% and 1.30 at ≥8% — and rapid swings in either direction carry independent risk (OR ≈ 2.4 for steep falls). A systematic review across post-PCI populations confirms the U-shape. The layman translation NURA exists for: chasing a "perfect low" number in a frail parent can be as dangerous as neglect — stability wins.
Red flagsHbA1c ≥8%, any value <5.5% in a treated diabetic, or a swing >1.5 points between tests; repeated low-sugar episodes (shakiness, sweats, confusion) are urgent medication-review triggers.
CadenceEvery 3 months until stable, then 6-monthly.
SourcesHbA1c change and post-AMI survival · Systematic review, post-PCI · U-shape in CVD+diabetes, Sci Reports

6 · Blood the carrying capacity

Hemoglobin

oxygen-carrying capacity — anemia makes every organ's recovery harder
RangesWHO anemia cut-offs: <13 g/dL (men), <12 (women).
EvidenceB Anemia in elderly cardiovascular patients independently predicts mortality (≈2× in several cohorts) and worsens heart-failure outcomes. Doubly relevant post-stent: blood-thinning dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) makes slow blood loss — and thus falling hemoglobin — a specific, watchable risk.
Red flagsA downward Hb trend on DAPT (with or without black stools, unusual fatigue, breathlessness) — bleeding check with the doctor. Iron studies (ferritin) explain most other anemia.
CadenceCBC every 3–6 months in year one post-stent, sooner with symptoms.
SourcesAnemia in elderly CVD · HALP score & mortality, NHANES

The "quiet" CBC indices — RDW, MCV, WBC, lymphocyte %

numbers on every report that almost no one reads — yet four of them help compute biological age
EvidenceB RDW (variation in red-cell size), MCV, white-cell count, and lymphocyte % are four of the nine inputs of PhenoAge (§8) — each independently associated with mortality in NHANES-scale cohorts. Elevated RDW in particular is a robust, underappreciated adverse-outcome marker in cardiac patients. NURA can mine value from data families already have, at zero extra testing cost.
SourcesLevine et al., PLOS Medicine

7 · Vitals & daily signals the free biomarkers measured at home

Blood pressure

the load on every vessel wall — the most consequential home measurement in existence
TargetsPost-event care generally aims <130/80; trials of intensive control (<120 systolic) show lower mortality and events at the cost of more side effects — the exact trade-off is the doctor's call, individualized for age and frailty.
EvidenceA A standard 10/5 mmHg reduction cuts major cardiovascular events ~28% in secondary prevention (meta-analysis); benefits hold in primary and secondary prevention alike.
Red flagsHome readings drifting up across a week; any reading >180/120 (urgent); dizziness on standing (possible overtreatment in the elderly — also a falls risk).
CadenceHome BP 2–3×/week when stable, daily after medication changes — morning, seated, rested. The weekly average trend is the datum, not any single reading.
SourcesHypertension meta-analysis 2024 · SBP <120 meta-analysis

Resting heart rate · SpO₂ · weight

three numbers a family can watch daily that genuinely forecast trouble
EvidenceB Resting HR >80 bpm carries RR 1.45 for all-cause mortality vs lower rates (1.2M-person meta-analysis); in cardiac patients, guideline practice treats sustained elevation as a modifiable target. A Rapid weight gain (~2 kg over 3 days) is the standard early-warning sign of fluid retention in heart-failure care — often days before breathlessness. SpO₂ <94% sustained (without known lung disease) merits medical review.
Red flagsResting HR creeping up week over week · 2 kg in 3 days · SpO₂ sagging below baseline — each is a "call the doctor today" trend, and each is capturable via a ₹2K weighing scale, a BP cuff, and a pulse oximeter.
SourcesResting HR meta-analysis, CMAJ · RHR in CVD patients

8 · Composite intelligence where NURA's product magic lives

PhenoAge — biological age from a routine blood panel

nine ordinary lab values + age, combined into "how old is this body really?"
InputsAlbumin · creatinine · glucose · CRP · alkaline phosphatase · lymphocyte % · MCV · RDW · WBC — all present in a standard Indian full-body panel a family already possesses.
EvidenceB Levine et al. (NHANES, validated out-of-sample): PhenoAge predicts all-cause mortality, disease onset, and physical function better than counting existing diseases. Applied in multivessel coronary-disease patients, phenotypic age predicts mortality. C Expanded biomarker sets improve prediction further — NURA's long-run direction as its data pool grows.
Product noteThis is NURA's signature computed insight: "Mom's biological age improved 1.8 years over six months of recovery" — hope, quantified from tests already done, at zero marginal cost. Framed always as a wellness trend, never a diagnosis.
SourcesLevine 2018, PLOS Medicine · PhenoAge in coronary disease · Biological age from blood biomarkers, Nature Comms Bio

Cardiac rehabilitation — the intervention the data should push toward

the single best-evidenced thing a recovering family can actually do
EvidenceA Cochrane/meta-analytic evidence (85 RCTs, 23,000+ patients): exercise-based cardiac rehab cuts cardiovascular mortality 26% (RR 0.74, number-needed-to-treat 37) and hospital readmissions ~18%. Participation in India is dismal — awareness, distance, cost. A family dashboard that tracks rehab adherence like a vital sign converts the strongest evidence in this entire document into daily behaviour.
SourcesCochrane review, JACC · 2023 meta-analysis, Eur Heart J

8b · Lifestyle levers the daily inputs that move every marker above

Biomarkers are outputs; lifestyle is the input side of the equation — and the levers below carry some of the largest effect sizes in all of post-op medicine. These power NURA's daily engagement loop: what the family logs each day is what the evidence says actually changes the trajectory.

Smoking cessation

the single largest survival lever a post-MI family controls
EvidenceA Meta-analysis of cohort studies (5,000+ post-MI patients): quitting after a heart attack cuts mortality ~46% (OR 0.54; number-needed-to-treat ≈ 13) — a larger effect than most medications in the regimen; benefit consistent across every subgroup studied. Across the literature, cessation reduces recurrent MI and death by 30–50%.
Product noteSmoking status is a day-one intake field and, for smokers, a tracked daily habit with milestone encouragement — never shame. Household smoking matters too (secondhand exposure).
SourcesJAMA Int Med meta-analysis · DARE quality-assessed review

Daily movement (steps)

the cheapest measurable habit with a mortality dose-response curve
EvidenceA Meta-analyses of 15–17 international cohorts: each +1,000 steps/day associates with ~15% lower all-cause mortality; for adults 60+, risk keeps falling up to ~6,000–8,000 steps/day (younger adults ~8–10K); benefits begin far below the mythical 10,000 — even ~2,600 vs 2,000 steps registers. A 2025 Lancet Public Health review suggests ~7,000 as the pragmatic target.
Product notePost-op walking progresses under the doctor/rehab plan — NURA tracks the trend and celebrates streaks; phone step counters make this the first automatically-capturable lifestyle input (no wearable required).
SourcesLancet Public Health, 15 cohorts · Eur J Prev Cardiol dose-response · 2025 dose-response review

Diet pattern (Mediterranean-style, adapted to Indian kitchens)

an eating pattern with trial evidence in people who already had a cardiac event
EvidenceA CORDIOPREV (secondary-prevention RCT): Mediterranean pattern cut major cardiovascular events ~27% vs a low-fat diet on top of optimal medication. B Lyon Diet Heart Study reported 50–70% fewer recurrent events (older, smaller trial). C Cochrane grades overall mortality evidence in secondary prevention as low-quality — honest read: pattern strongly promising, effect size uncertain. Mechanisms (LDL oxidation, inflammation) align with the hs-CRP/LDL entries above. India translation: mustard-oil/nuts/legumes/vegetable-forward thali mapping, not olive-oil dogma.
Product noteCaptured as weekly photo-sampled meals + pattern score, not calorie policing. Dietitian referral, not prescription.
SourcesUpdated systematic review · Cochrane review · Umbrella review

10 · From evidence to engine — how the product consumes this document

  1. Curated rules, not model opinions. Each entry above becomes structured rules: marker × context (post-PCI, on-DAPT, diabetic, elderly) × threshold/trend → insight template + evidence grade + citation. The AI model's job is extraction (reading reports) and translation (explaining in the family's language) — the medicine comes from this vetted base, so the same input always yields the same insight. That is the difference between NURA and a chatbot, and it is also the safety architecture.
  2. Every insight shows its work. Grade badge + source link on each card in the product. Trust is the product; citations are the UI of trust.
  3. Trend primitives first. The engine's v1 vocabulary: at-target / off-target vs context-specific goals · direction over last 3 values · rate-of-change alarms (creatinine ≥25%, weight 2kg/3d, Hb down-trend on DAPT, K⁺ out of band, HbA1c swings) · monitoring-cadence overdue reminders. These cover the highest-value post-op moments with fully deterministic logic.
  4. The doctor stays the hero. Outputs are framed as preparation: "worth discussing", "ask about", "bring this trend chart." A junior doctor reading a NURA summary should find it clinically literate and properly humble.
